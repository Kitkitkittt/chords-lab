# Formative Assessment and Review Integrity

Research resolution for [Research formative assessment and review integrity](https://github.com/Kitkitkittt/chords-lab/issues/6).

## Decision-relevant findings

- **Lesson completion and retained learning are different signals.** Retrieval practice improves delayed recall; repeated study after a correct response does not provide the same benefit. Exposure or completion, attempted retrieval, and later retained performance should remain distinct. [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/)
- **Answer reveal is feedback, not evidence of unaided knowledge.** Testing supports retention and corrective feedback reduces multiple-choice error persistence. A response after a hint or reveal must not be recorded as an unaided correct retrieval. [Roediger & Karpicke, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/) · [Butler & Roediger, 2008](https://doi.org/10.3758/MC.36.3.604)
- **Failed attempts can contribute to learning when followed by corrective feedback.** An incorrect first response is useful learning data, not a reason for punishment or lockout. Later retrieval—not an immediate post-reveal response—is the meaningful retention check. [Kornell, Hays & Bjork, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015729)
- **Review should be spaced without one hard-coded universal interval.** Distributed practice has a robust benefit, while useful gaps depend on the desired retention interval. Scheduling should preserve retrieval history, remain adjustable, and make missed reviews available without debt or punishment. [Cepeda et al., 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354) · [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x) · [IES practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Docs/PracticeGuide/20072004.pdf)
- **Confidence is self-report, not mastery evidence.** Performance predictions may not correlate with later performance. Confidence should remain separate from correctness and retention, optional, and non-gating. [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/)
- **Avoid contingent completion and performance rewards.** Expected tangible engagement-, completion-, and performance-contingent rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation, while positive informational feedback can support it. This supports ChordsLab's no-streak and no-pressure principles. [Deci, Koestner & Ryan, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)
- **Preserve autonomy and accessible feedback.** Official UDL guidance supports learner choice, action-oriented feedback, and self-monitoring rather than forced pacing or compulsory reflection. [CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/)

## Constraints for downstream decisions

1. Represent exposure or completion, unaided first response, support or reveal before response, feedback delivered, later review response, and optional confidence as distinct evidence.
2. Reveal or hint remains an escape from friction, but makes that attempt assisted and cannot upgrade unaided retrieval evidence.
3. Corrective feedback provides the correct answer and an actionable correction. Immediate retry remains practice rather than proof of retention.
4. Permit retry and return later. Do not impose timers, lockout-producing attempt caps, streaks, overdue penalties, or completion/performance rewards.
5. Schedule review from retrieval history over time, with adjustable interval policy and neutral availability language.
6. Keep confidence optional, neutral, and separate from correctness. Never gate or reward on it.
7. Treat learner-facing progress claims as interpretations that require multiple evidence points, not facts derived from one interaction. [Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html)

## Non-findings and limits

No primary evidence located selects exact retry limits, reveal timing, confidence scale, wording, or review intervals for beginner chord learning. Most core studies concern adults, university learners, or verbal memory; transfer to musical perceptual and motor learning and ADHD-first use is plausible but unvalidated here. The evidence supports guardrails, not one exact adaptive algorithm.

## Confidence

Moderate to high for retrieval, feedback, spacing, autonomy, and non-punitive guardrails; low for exact interaction policy.
